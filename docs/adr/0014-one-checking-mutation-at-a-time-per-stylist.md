# One checking mutation at a time per Stylist

Mutations that read Appointment or Client state and then write depending on what they read run in one database transaction that starts by taking a Postgres advisory lock keyed by the Stylist (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`, released when the transaction ends). They use `stylistTransactionProcedure` in `packages/api/src/trpc.ts`, which puts the transaction on `ctx.db`. Today these are Appointment Creation, Appointment changes, adding and removing Clients on an Appointment, adding or changing a Service, adding a Color Formula or an Appointment Photo, Appointment Copying, saving, deleting and merging a Client Profile, saying that two Clients are different people, creating a Profile Share or changing what it shows, saving a Service Menu Item, and saving the Stylist's settings.

The transaction makes each of them all-or-nothing: a refusal or a failure part-way leaves nothing behind, such as the inline Client of a refused Appointment Creation or half of a Client Profile deletion. The lock keeps two of them from interleaving for the same Stylist: the second waits, and because the transaction runs at read committed isolation, it then reads what the first committed. That is what keeps an Appointment from losing its last Client, a primary Client from being a Client who was just removed, and a Service from being added for a Client who is being removed. It is also what lets a creating mutation recognise a request that is sent again, keeps a Client at one active Profile Share, keeps the Services total of an Appointment within the largest amount, and lets a save be refused when the record changed after the form loaded it (ADR 0015).

We chose one lock per Stylist over row locks on each Appointment and Client. Every record belongs to exactly one Stylist, a solo Stylist produces almost no concurrent writes, and several of these mutations touch many rows at once (deleting a Client Profile, saving the salon address). A single lock taken first cannot deadlock and needs no lock-ordering rules. The cost is three more database round trips per such mutation and no parallel checking mutations for one Stylist.

A check only counts when it is read through `ctx.db` inside the procedure; `ctx.stylist` is read before the lock, so a procedure that depends on a Stylist field reads it again. Mutations that are a single statement, that change or delete one Color Formula or Appointment Photo row, that delete one Service, or that revoke a Profile Share do not take the lock. When one of them loses a race with a delete, the base procedure answers the database's foreign-key, missing-record, or unique-constraint failure as `NOT_FOUND` or `CONFLICT` instead of an internal error.
