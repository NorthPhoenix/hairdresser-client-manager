# One checking mutation at a time per Stylist

Mutations that read Appointment or Client state and then write depending on what they read run in one database transaction that starts by taking a Postgres advisory lock keyed by the Stylist (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`, released when the transaction ends). They use `stylistTransactionProcedure` in `packages/api/src/trpc.ts`, which puts the transaction on `ctx.db`. Today these are Appointment Creation, Appointment changes, adding and removing Clients on an Appointment, adding a Service or an Appointment Photo, Appointment Copying, Client Profile deletion, and saving the Stylist's settings.

The transaction makes each of them all-or-nothing: a refusal or a failure part-way leaves nothing behind, such as the inline Client of a refused Appointment Creation or half of a Client Profile deletion. The lock keeps two of them from interleaving for the same Stylist: the second waits, and because the transaction runs at read committed isolation, it then reads what the first committed. That is what keeps an Appointment from losing its last Client, a primary Client from being a Client who was just removed, and a Service from being added for a Client who is being removed.

We chose one lock per Stylist over row locks on each Appointment and Client. Every record belongs to exactly one Stylist, a solo Stylist produces almost no concurrent writes, and several of these mutations touch many rows at once (deleting a Client Profile, saving the salon address). A single lock taken first cannot deadlock and needs no lock-ordering rules. The cost is three more database round trips per such mutation and no parallel checking mutations for one Stylist.

A check only counts when it is read through `ctx.db` inside the procedure; `ctx.stylist` is read before the lock, so a procedure that depends on a Stylist field reads it again. Mutations that are a single statement, or that change one Service, Color Formula, or Appointment Photo row, do not take the lock. When one of them loses a race with a delete, the base procedure answers the database's foreign-key, missing-record, or unique-constraint failure as `NOT_FOUND` or `CONFLICT` instead of an internal error.
